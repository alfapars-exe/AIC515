\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{What the box label rewards.} Three independent lines of evidence point the same way. The same predictions lose 0.55 AUPRC when scored against the pixels that actually changed (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-h1}); the box model is indifferent to blurring the box interior but not its rim; and a box detector trained on the same labels reaches 0.969 test AUPRC under the box metric, more than any segmenter, while scoring 0.368 under the pixel-change truth. A benchmark that scores localization against the edited field's box therefore measures \emph{field detection}. That is a useful capability for triage, and box detection delivers it with the highest box scores, but it should not be reported as pixel-level tamper localization.

\textbf{The marker is a shortcut, not the whole story.} Pasting only the residual green line onto an authentic image is enough for the standard box model to find the field, and its test AP drops from 0.97 on images that carry the residue to 0.69 on images that do not. Yet box models retrained on marker-repaired images (three seeds) keep 0.912 validation AUPRC (0.923 before) and score similarly on residue-free Gemini images (0.762 vs.\ 0.722, overlapping CIs). The residue is used when present and replaced by other cues when removed, which means the headline AIForge-Doc numbers overstate the difficulty-adjusted performance on marker-bearing images, but do not consist of the marker alone. Since the residue is present in 86\,\% of Gemini and 2\,\% of Ideogram forgeries, held-out-generator results on v1 confound generator and marker; repairing the marker is a prerequisite for any generator-transfer claim on this dataset. The dependence is not specific to localizers: an image-level patch classifier loses half of its detections when the residue is repaired, and attributions of all four examined models concentrate on the residue pixels (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-cls} and~\ref{sec:res-xai}). The marker-free v2 benchmark confirms this from outside: models trained with the marker lose most on exactly the specifications that carried it, and the repaired box model loses nothing on the others (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-v2}).

\textbf{Recommendations for document-forgery benchmarks.} (i) Release the authentic source with every training forgery, as AIForge-Doc does, and report a pixel-change metric next to the box metric. (ii) Remove generation-pipeline annotations (markers, crop borders) from the released images and audit residue with a simple colour test. (iii) Report box-level AP separately from pixel metrics, with a box detector as a baseline. (iv) Report leakage-free test subsets and group-aware confidence intervals.

\textbf{Limitations.} The pixel-change ground truth is derived automatically ($\varepsilon{=}8$ with morphology). A vision-language check rated it OK in 69 of 96 audited pairs, but a stricter human audit of the same stratified 100 pairs by the author rated it partial in 64 and wrong in none: in all 64 the auditor's note describes over-coverage, the mask also marking re-rendered background or unchanged content of the field (WildReceipt 39/39 partial, XFUND 0/11), and the two audits agree poorly (Cohen's $\kappa{=}0.07$, 95\,\% CI $[-0.08,0.21]$). The same audit found the official box correct in 99 of 100 pairs. \GTp{} therefore over-covers rather than misses the edit; the human audit has a single annotator, the author, so no inter-annotator agreement is available. The second architecture, the marker-repaired pixel and glyph models, the joint glyph model, the 20-epoch runs and the YOLO ablations use one seed; the main U-Net grid, the marker-repaired box model, the joint pixel model, the held-out-generator U-Nets, the main YOLO model and the patch classifier use three. Ideogram contributes 126 held-out images. TruFor could not be fine-tuned because its training code is not released, so learned in-domain models are compared with zero-shot references only. Image-level detection is measured on validation pairs because the test authentic set is not paired. The patch classifier is the exception: its threshold is calibrated on validation pairs and tested against the 89 paired authentic test images, so its test false-positive rate has a wide interval; its thresholds lie close to saturation (0.94--0.995) and vary with the seed; it was not evaluated on v2, under degradations or with a held-out generator. The attribution analysis covers eight inputs at a low evaluation budget. The external test on AIForge-Doc~v2 uses models trained on v1 only; the marker-repaired pixel and glyph models there have one seed, and v2 changes both the generator and the paste-back procedure, which our same-specification comparison separates only through the v1 residue split.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
On AI-inpainted financial documents, the official box masks train and reward detectors of the edited field, not localizers of the changed pixels: changing only the training mask reverses the model ranking, the effect replicates across decoders, and a tiled box detector is the best model under the box metric and transfers best across generators, yet localizes changed pixels no better than the box-trained U-Net. A residual edit marker in most Gemini forgeries is exploited when present, by the segmenters, more weakly by the detector and most strongly by an image-level patch classifier; retraining without it preserves most of the box score but removes the marker-specific advantage. Pixel-change supervision gives the most faithful masks and the fewest false alarms, but it does not close the generator gap (joint supervision was not tested on the held-out generator). On the marker-free AIForge-Doc v2, the segmenters trained on marker-bearing v1 images lose 43--64\,\% of their box AUPRC, the marker-repaired box model (24\,\%) and the tiled detector (8\,\%) far less. We recommend reporting pixel-change and box metrics side by side, auditing generation residue, and treating held-out-generator results on marker-bearing data with caution.
