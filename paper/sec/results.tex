\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{H1: the ground truth decides the ranking}
\label{sec:res-h1}
Table~\ref{tab:dualgt} scores every model under every ground truth. With the U-Net, the \GTb-trained model reaches a pooled AUPRC of 0.923 under \GTb{} but 0.374 under \GTp{} and 0.257 under \GTg; on the official test set the same predictions give 0.950 under \GTb{} and 0.427 under \GTp{} ($n{=}89$ pairs). Each ground truth ranks the model trained on it first, and the \GTb{} ranking is the reverse of the \GTg{} ranking. DeepLabV3+ reproduces the pattern with nearly identical numbers ($0.929 \rightarrow 0.382$, three seeds), so the effect is a property of the supervision, not of the U-Net decoder. Under its own metric the \GTb{} model is the best localizer in the study; under a pixel-change metric it is worse than a model that never saw a box. Most of the 0.92 is therefore earned by labelling unchanged pixels inside the rectangle.

\subsection{Shortcut and trace controls (H2, H4)}
\label{sec:res-controls}
The \GTb{} model predicts rectangles (median rectangularity 0.87 vs.\ 0.32 for \GTp{} and 0.05 for \GTg) and is indifferent to blurring the box interior (relative AUPRC change $+1.2\,\%$) while a 5-px blur of the box rim costs $-2.0\,\%$ (placebo ring 0.0\,\%; three seeds): it uses the field's extent more than its content, but the boundary seam is only a small cue (H2 partly supported). A ResNet-18 trained on 224-px crops that never touch the box separates forged from paired authentic images at chance (AUROC 0.516; 0.980 for box-centred crops through the same pipeline), so no global re-encoding trace is exploitable (H4 rejected). Benign paste-back and full-image resampling, which composite or resample without changing content, reduce image-level AUROC (99th-percentile score) by 1.6--3.0 points for the three-seed U-Nets and by at most 3.7 points for the other standard segmenters, but by 3.6--6.5 points for the marker-repaired models (all three seeds). The detector, scored by its maximum box confidence, loses at most 0.3 points (AUROC 0.995 against authentic images, 0.992 and 0.994 against the two controls; seed 42), whereas the box U-Net loses 2.2--2.3 points under the same maximum-score rule. The \GTp{} model raises far fewer false alarms on authentic images than the \GTb{} model (12\,\% vs.\ 26\,\% of images with $>$0.1\,\% positive pixels at 0.5; three seeds), and the joint model of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-variants} 10\,\%.

\subsection{The marker shortcut, and retraining without it}
\label{sec:res-marker}
Fig.~\ref{fig:probes} defines the input probes; Table~\ref{tab:marker} reports them on the 380 validation pairs whose forgery carries marker residue ($\ge$20\,px after a one-pixel dilation of the residue mask; 368 pairs without the dilation). All rows are three-seed means. For the standard box U-Net, copying only the residue pixels back from the authentic image lowers per-image AP from 0.988 to 0.766 ($\Delta{=}-0.222$ \ci{-0.256}{-0.190}), and pasting only the residue onto the authentic image makes the model cover 80\,\% of the box at $\tau^*$ (AP 0.905). DeepLabV3+ and the pixel-trained U-Net show the same dependence ($\Delta$ $-0.173$ and $-0.247$). On the official test set the box model's AP is 0.972 on the 627 images with residue and 0.690 on the 185 without (0.722 on the 102 residue-free Gemini images), so the gap is not only a generator gap.

Retraining on marker-repaired images removes the dependence without destroying the benchmark score. Over three seeds the repaired box model loses nothing when the residue is removed at test time ($\Delta{=}+0.017$ \ci{0.001}{0.033}); the residue alone now covers 44\,\% of the box instead of 80\,\% (10\,\% instead of 42\,\% for the pixel model). Its validation AUPRC is $0.912{\pm}0.006$ ($0.923{\pm}0.015$ before) and its test AUPRC $0.940{\pm}0.003$ ($0.950{\pm}0.011$); paired per image, the retraining costs 0.04 AP on both splits (validation $-0.038$ \ci{-0.054}{-0.021}, test $-0.040$ \ci{-0.052}{-0.029}). Its AP on residue images falls from 0.972 to 0.914, while on residue-free Gemini images it is similar (0.762 vs.\ 0.722, overlapping CIs). The marker is thus a shortcut the models take when it is offered, not a prerequisite for high box scores. The repaired pixel and glyph models also lose nothing when the residue is removed ($+0.054$ \ci{0.037}{0.072} and $+0.009$ \ci{0.002}{0.016}), but pay more for it: validation AUPRC under their own masks is $0.507{\pm}0.045$ and $0.644{\pm}0.014$ ($0.610$ and $0.676$ before), with one weak pixel seed (0.458).

\subsection{Re-rendering versus content}
\label{sec:res-render}
Generators re-render the whole field, so inside the box many pixels change by less than the \GTp{} threshold. Separating the two components on all validation pairs (Table~\ref{tab:render}) shows that neither alone reproduces the response to a real forgery. For the standard box U-Net (three seeds) the mean probability inside the box falls from 0.86 (forged) to 0.24 (render-only) and 0.35 (content-only), and forged-vs-authentic AUROC from 0.971 to 0.637 and 0.733: the standard models respond most strongly to the conjunction of re-rendering, changed glyphs and marker. Removing the marker from training shifts the models toward content: content-only AUROC rises to 0.886 for the box model (three seeds; 0.733 before) and to 0.851 for the pixel model (0.681 before; both three seeds), while their render-only response stays lower (0.724, 0.655). Content changes, not the generator's texture, become the main cue once the shortcut is gone.

\subsection{Held-out generator (H3), now with three seeds}
\label{sec:res-heldout}
Training on Gemini only and testing on the Ideogram edits of validation and test ($n{=}126$ under \GTb, 41 pairs under \GTp) confirms our earlier single-seed result with three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:heldout}). Every Gemini-only model collapses on Ideogram; the \GTb{} model falls from 0.909 to 0.412 per-image AP and the \GTp{} model from 0.567 to 0.264 (both scored under \GTb). The relative drop is smaller for pixel supervision, but its absolute Ideogram score is lower, and under its own ground truth the pixel model is no better on Ideogram than the box model (0.279 vs.\ 0.259). Pixel-change supervision does not, by itself, buy generator transfer. Because Ideogram forgeries almost never carry the marker (green residue in the box ring of 2.1\,\% of Ideogram vs.\ 85.9\,\% of Gemini forgeries), part of this gap could be a marker gap. To separate the two we retrained the Gemini-only models on marker-repaired images (three seeds; predictions committed before the run). The repaired Gemini-only box model no longer depends on the residue (repair $\Delta{=}+0.009$ \ci{-0.004}{0.023}, against $-0.275$ for the standard Gemini-only model) but still collapses on Ideogram: 0.463 against 0.874 on matched Gemini. Paired per image it gains $0.051$ \ci{0.004}{0.096} on Ideogram and loses $0.035$ \ci{0.023}{0.047} on Gemini; the pixel and glyph models behave alike ($+0.053$ and $+0.037$ on Ideogram). The marker accounts for about a sixth of the box model's gap (0.497 $\rightarrow$ 0.412); the rest is specific to the generator.

\subsection{Supervision variants, training length and robustness}
\label{sec:res-variants}
\emph{Joint supervision} (pixel mask plus an auxiliary box channel, three seeds) is the only variant that improves the pixel model, and it does so mainly under the box metric: validation AUPRC under \GTb{} rises from $0.579{\pm}0.014$ to $0.668{\pm}0.019$ and test \GTb{} AUPRC from $0.638{\pm}0.015$ to $0.702{\pm}0.020$ (paired per-image AP $+0.051$ \ci{0.043}{0.058} and $+0.046$ \ci{0.040}{0.052}). Under \GTp{} the gain is small and confined to validation: $0.633{\pm}0.013$ against $0.610{\pm}0.012$ (per-image AP $+0.011$ \ci{0.005}{0.017}), while on the 89 test pairs it is absent ($0.652{\pm}0.003$ vs.\ $0.654{\pm}0.016$; $+0.004$ \ci{-0.008}{0.015}). The blur penalty shrinks from $-59$\,\% to $-46$\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:dualgt}; blur from the robustness runs), and image-level AUROC against authentic images is $0.905{\pm}0.008$ against $0.892{\pm}0.006$. The joint model still depends on the marker ($\Delta{=}-0.194$, Table~\ref{tab:marker}).
For the glyph model the auxiliary box channel brings nothing (\GTg{} $0.641{\pm}0.063$ vs.\ $0.676{\pm}0.019$). \emph{Twenty epochs} do not help under any mask (three seeds): \GTb{} $0.912{\pm}0.031$ (vs.\ $0.923$), \GTp{} $0.591{\pm}0.015$ (vs.\ $0.610$), \GTg{} $0.598{\pm}0.082$ (vs.\ $0.676$), and the glyph model becomes unstable across seeds; the concern that ten epochs were too few is not supported.
\emph{Test-time preprocessing.} Converting the input to grayscale is the most damaging operation we tested, costing 50--67\,\% of the AUPRC of every segmenter for which it was measured (DeepLabV3+, joint and marker-repaired models) and 58\,\% for the detector, so colour carries evidence beyond the green line. CLAHE changes AUPRC by at most 3\,\%. JPEG Q75, $0.75\times$ rescaling and $\sigma{=}1$ blur hurt pixel and glyph models more than box models (e.g.\ blur $-59$\,\% vs.\ $-21$\,\% for the U-Net), and the marker-repaired models are less robust to JPEG ($-33$ to $-50$\,\%) than their standard counterparts.

\subsection{Box detection versus segmentation}
\label{sec:res-yolo}
A tiled YOLO11s trained on the same boxes is the strongest model in the study \emph{under the box metric} and is no better than the box U-Net under the pixel metric (Tables~\ref{tab:dualgt} and~\ref{tab:yolo}). Over three seeds it reaches 0.965 validation and 0.969 test AUPRC under \GTb{} (U-Net 0.923 / 0.950), test F1@$\tau^*$ 0.946 and IoU 0.918 (U-Net 0.846 / 0.789) and box AP@0.5 0.880 (U-Net 0.873 for seeds 42/43; 0.391 for seed 44, whose calibrated $\tau^*{=}0.95$ fragments the predicted region; marker-repaired U-Net $0.804{\pm}0.005$ over three seeds), while its \GTp{} AUPRC is 0.368 (U-Net \GTb{} model 0.374). The detector depends far less on the marker: its test AP on the 185 residue-free images is 0.878 (U-Net 0.690) and 0.886 on residue-free Gemini images (0.722). It also transfers better across generators: trained on all tools it reaches 0.872 AP on Ideogram (U-Net 0.629; both three seeds), and trained on Gemini only it keeps 0.725 (U-Net 0.412; Gemini 0.938 vs.\ 0.909; three seeds each). The probes (three seeds) suggest why: removing the residue costs the detector only 0.058 AP (U-Net 0.222), whereas the \emph{render-only} input, which keeps the generator's re-rendering but no changed glyph, is still separated from authentic images with AUROC 0.890 (U-Net 0.637). The detector has learned what a regenerated field looks like, a cue that is shared by both generators but says nothing about which pixels changed.

The ablations (three seeds each) are only partly as expected. Resizing whole images to 1280 instead of tiling at native resolution costs 0.033 test AUPRC and 0.128 AP on residue-free images, but \emph{raises} box AP@0.5 from 0.88 to 0.96 (0.93 on fields below 1\,\% of the image): a plausible cause is that fields cut at tile borders yield partial boxes that cross-tile NMS does not merge, although the untiled detector also emits about four boxes per image, so the mechanism is not established; tiling helps coverage but not box precision here; model size matters little (n/s/m: 0.966/0.969/0.966 test AUPRC); Ultralytics' default augmentation is as good as the conservative profile (0.973), contrary to our expectation that mosaic and scale jitter would destroy forensic traces; one extra authentic tile per image changes nothing (0.969). The detector is insensitive to blur, CLAHE and rescaling ($\le$1.3\,\% AUPRC) but loses 45\,\% under JPEG Q75 and 58\,\% under grayscale. Contrary to our expectation, detection was not cheaper in our implementation: full-image inference takes 76--98\,ms for the tiled n/s/m detectors, 303\,ms for the 1280 detector, 81\,ms for the U-Net and 122\,ms for DeepLabV3+ (mean 3.1\,MP per image), because per-tile pre- and post-processing dominates.
