\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Image-editing services built on multimodal generators can now rewrite a single amount on a receipt or a field on a form without a visible seam. Detectors trained on classical splicing or on synthetic document tampering transfer poorly to this setting: on AIForge-Doc~v1~\cite{wu2026aiforgedoc}, the generic localizer TruFor~\cite{guillaro2023trufor} reaches a pixel IoU of 0.36 zero-shot and document-specific models fall further, and on its successor humans perform at chance~\cite{wu2026aiforgedocv2}. The obvious response is to train localizers on these benchmarks. We ask a prior question: \emph{what does the ground truth of such a benchmark actually teach a localizer, and what do its scores measure?}

AIForge-Doc annotates every forgery with the bounding box of the edited field. Our audit of the released files shows that every mask is a rectangle, that only a median 49\,\% of the box pixels differ from the paired authentic image, and that one third of the forgeries also change pixels outside the box. A segmentation network trained on such masks is rewarded for a rectangle around any detectable cue, which is the setting in which shortcut learning thrives~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}. Annotation geometry has been questioned for natural-image edits~\cite{shang2026maskspixels}, but not for AI-inpainted documents and not with controlled training. AIForge-Doc releases the authentic source of every training forgery, which lets us derive a pixel-change ground truth and change \emph{only} the training mask.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A dual-ground-truth protocol.} The same localizer trained on box, pixel-change and glyph masks (three seeds each) is scored under all three; the box-trained model's 0.92 validation AUPRC drops to 0.37 under the pixel-change truth and each ground truth ranks its own model first (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-h1}). The effect replicates with a second decoder (DeepLabV3+).
\item \textbf{A hidden marker shortcut, removed and re-measured.} The generation pipeline leaves a 1--3\,px green line of the drawn edit marker in over 80\,\% of Gemini forgeries. Pasting only this line onto an authentic image makes the box model ``find'' the field; we retrain every condition on marker-repaired images (three seeds each) and report how much of the benchmark score survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-marker}).
\item \textbf{A region-appearance decomposition} that separates the content change from the generator's re-rendering of the field (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-render}).
\item \textbf{Box detection versus segmentation.} A tiled YOLO11 detector trained on the same boxes, scored with the same code under both ground truths and with box AP, beats every segmenter under the box metric (test AUPRC 0.969), relies less on the marker and transfers better across generators, yet scores like the box U-Net under the pixel-change truth (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-yolo}).
\item \textbf{The shortcut in a third model family and in attributions.} An image-level ResNet-18 patch classifier (three seeds) reaches 0.978 test AUROC once its decision threshold is calibrated, yet misses half of its detections when the residue is repaired and flags 96\,\% of authentic images that carry only the residue; SHAP attributions concentrate on residue pixels (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-cls} and~\ref{sec:res-xai}).
\item \textbf{Negative results} on the paste-back seam, a global re-encoding trace, held-out-generator transfer with pixel supervision or after marker repair (three seeds), longer training and test-time enhancement.
\end{enumerate}
We release the audit and the code that derives the masks, controls and probes and evaluates the models\footnote{\url{https://github.com/alfapars-exe/AIC515}}; the images and the masks derived from them are not redistributed and are regenerated from the gated dataset by this code.
