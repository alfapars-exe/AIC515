## 1. Introduction

Commercial image-editing services built on diffusion and multimodal generators (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, Ideogram v2 Edit, GPT-Image-2) can rewrite a single numeric field of a receipt or a form while leaving no visible seam. Recent benchmarks show that forensic detectors trained on classical splicing or on synthetic document tampering transfer poorly to this threat. On AIForge-Doc v1 the generic localizer TruFor reaches an image-level AUC of 0.751 and a pixel IoU of 0.358 zero-shot, while the document-specific DocTamper/DTD model drops to an AUC of 0.563 and an IoU of 0.020 [@wu2026aiforgedoc]; on AIForge-Doc v2 (GPT-Image-2) human observers are at chance (2AFC accuracy 0.501) and TruFor reaches only 0.599 [@wu2026aiforgedocv2]; across eight document test sets none of fourteen published localizers reaches a pixel-F1 of 0.3 on six or more sets without adaptation [@zhao2026docforgebench]. The natural response is to fine-tune a localizer on the new datasets. This paper asks a prior question: **what does the ground truth of such a dataset actually teach a localizer, and what does the reported accuracy actually measure?**

AIForge-Doc annotates every forgery with the *bounding box of the edited field*, not with the pixels that changed. Auditing the released files we found that these masks are rectangles without exception, that at a difference threshold of 8 grey levels only a median 49 % of the pixels inside the box differ from the released authentic image, and that one third of the forgeries also change pixels *outside* the box. A segmentation network trained on such masks is rewarded for predicting a rectangle around any detectable glyph change, so the paste-back geometry can become a *shortcut* in the sense of Geirhos et al. [@geirhos2020shortcut] — a decision rule that fits the training distribution without capturing the intended evidence. Shortcut reliance of document localizers is well documented for the JPEG 8×8 grid: ADCD-Net reports a 68–73 % F1 drop of DTD under a one-pixel shift [@wong2025adcdnet] and DocQT a 14.5-point F1 effect of the quantization table alone [@docqt2026]. No study has examined the effect of the *annotation geometry* on what is learned, nor measured any of these effects on AI-inpainted documents. During our audit we additionally found that three quarters of the Gemini-generated forgeries carry a thin saturated-green line along the inside edge of the annotated box — a residue of the rectangle marker the dataset pipeline draws before inpainting — which turns out to be a near-deterministic cue for the box location.

AIForge-Doc is unusual in that the authentic source image is released for every training forgery. This lets us derive a second, *pixel-change* ground truth from paired differences and run a controlled comparison: the same architecture, data, augmentation and budget, with only the training mask changed. The study was conducted in two pre-registered phases (a literature and gap analysis with falsifiable hypotheses, then the experiments), and all code, derived masks and result tables are released. Our contributions are:

1. **A reproducible audit of AIForge-Doc v1** (12,187 files) quantifying mask semantics, outside-box edits, a global re-encoding trace, source-document leakage between official splits (8.9 % of test forgeries), the invalidity of the released test "authentic" set for image-level evaluation, and a previously unreported green marker residue present in 75 % of forged–authentic pairs (Sec. 3).
2. **Derived pixel-change and glyph ground truths** for all 3,247 verified training pairs with an ε-sensitivity analysis, a benign-control set that reproduces the pipeline's compositing step without content change, and a 100-pair verification sheet audited with a vision-language model (Sec. 4).
3. **A shortcut-audit protocol** for box-annotated localizers: dual-ground-truth evaluation, boundary ablation with placebo and interior controls, rectangle-fit post-processing, benign controls, a global-trace probe on clean crops, and a marker-transfer/marker-repair probe (Sec. 5.7).
4. **Full-data experiments**: nine ResNet-18 U-Nets (three ground truths × three seeds) plus Gemini-only runs, evaluated on the official test split, under degradations and on a held-out generator, with group-bootstrap confidence intervals and Holm-corrected decisions for four pre-registered hypotheses, alongside zero-shot TruFor and ADCD-Net references under identical evaluation code (Sec. 6).

Section 2 reviews related work, Section 3 the dataset audit, Section 4 the derived ground truths and controls, Section 5 the methods and the reasons for choosing them, Section 6 the results, Section 7 the discussion and threats to validity, Section 8 what was solved and what remains open, and Section 9 the reproducibility package.

## 2. Related Work

**Document tampering localization.** DocTamper/DTD [@qu2023doctamper] introduced a 170k-image synthetic benchmark (copy-move, splicing and typeset "generation" of text) and a dual-stream network that fuses RGB with 8×8 block-DCT coefficients and the JPEG quantization table, trained with a compression-quality curriculum. FFDN [@chen2024ffdn] injects the same frequency cues through a zero-initialised module and a wavelet decomposition so that they cannot dominate the RGB stream; ADCD-Net [@wong2025adcdnet] gates the DCT stream with a predicted block-alignment score and adds OCR-based text/background disentanglement; DiffNet [@dhouib2026diffnet] makes the DCT backbone efficient and reports the best cross-domain average on the ForensicHub document protocol. All of these train on the DocTamper synthesis and rely on JPEG evidence. DOCFORGE-BENCH [@zhao2026docforgebench] evaluates fourteen detectors zero-shot on eight document sets and attributes most failures to threshold calibration when tampered pixels are 0.27–4 % of the image; DocQT [@docqt2026] and DiffNet explicitly name compression-profile and synthesis-pipeline shortcuts. Earlier and parallel work on tampered scene and e-commerce text (Tampered-IC13 [@wang2022tamperedic13], the ICDAR 2023 competition [@luo2023icdar], RTM [@luo2025rtm], OSTF/DAF with its open-set generator protocol [@qu2025ostf], CTP-Net [@ctpnet2023], TextSleuth [@qu2024textsleuth], FSTS [@fsts2025], DITL² [@li2025ditl2], forgery-trace enhancement [@dong2024tce]) uses box- or pixel-level labels of human or programmatic edits; none addresses API inpainting.

**AI-generated document forgeries.** AIForge-Doc v1 [@wu2026aiforgedoc] and v2 [@wu2026aiforgedocv2] are, to our knowledge, the only public pixel-annotated benchmarks of API-inpainted financial documents; both evaluate detectors zero-shot only and both describe a context-window inpainting pipeline in which a 3 px green rectangle marker is drawn on the crop and only the field box is pasted back. A concurrent study on a third round [@raj2026images25] replaces the drawn marker with an alpha mask, pastes back the whole context crop, and reports that TruFor's maps score as well against the pasted crop rectangle as against the field box (pixel AUC 0.921 vs 0.918 on 200 rows), concluding that a splice localizer can score well on that round without localizing the edit. That finding concerns a paste-back seam in round v3 and zero-shot detectors; it does not examine v1, trained localizers, the box-versus-pixel ground truth or the marker residue studied here. The Receipt/Find-it dataset [@martineztornes2023receipt] provides human receipt forgeries; GPT4o-Receipt [@zhang2026gpt4oreceipt] and the MLLM study of [@mllmdoc2025] show that fully generated receipts are often detectable from arithmetic inconsistency rather than pixels; TextFake [@textfake2026] documents a "text-density curse" for low-level detectors. TGIF [@mareen2024tgif] shows that when an inpainting model re-decodes the whole image, classical splicing localizers collapse (F1 ≤ 0.1), and COCO-Inpaint [@cocoinpaint2025] provides cross-generator splits for natural images.

**Generic manipulation localization.** TruFor [@guillaro2023trufor] fuses a learned noise residual (Noiseprint++) with RGB and outputs a confidence map; CAT-Net [@kwon2022catnet] learns double-JPEG statistics; MVSS-Net [@dong2023mvssnet] combines a noise view with a Sobel edge view; PSCC-Net [@liu2022psccnet], IML-ViT [@ma2023imlvit], Mesorch [@zhu2025mesorch], SAFIRE [@kwon2025safire] and Omni-IML [@qu2024omniiml] represent progressive, transformer, multi-scale, promptable and unified designs. Several of these supervise edges explicitly, i.e., they *use* boundary artefacts.

**Shortcuts, generalization and evaluation reliability.** Geirhos et al. [@geirhos2020shortcut] define shortcut learning and recommend out-of-distribution testing by default; GenD [@yermakov2026gend] shows that unpaired real/fake training alone can move cross-dataset deepfake AUROC from 56.8 to 90.0; Smeu et al. [@smeu2025shortcuts] expose a trivial dataset artefact (leading silence) in audio-visual deepfake benchmarks; frequency bias is identified as a root cause of poor generalization in [@freqbias2025]; Aoudi et al. [@aoudi2026beyond] describe a "metric trap" in natural-image forgery localization where pixel agreement hides reliance on boundary artefacts; GIFTBench [@giftbench2026] separates source, target and operation shifts; BR-Gen/NFA-ViT [@cai2026zooming] targets small localized AI edits. Our work transfers these concerns to the geometry of document annotations and to a concrete pipeline artefact.

**Classical image processing and learning components.** The pipeline follows the course material: 2-D block DCT [@ahmed1974dct], local binary patterns [@ojala2002lbp], grey-level co-occurrence features [@haralick1973glcm], Sauvola adaptive binarization [@sauvola2000], support vector machines [@cortes1995svm] and random forests [@breiman2001rf], residual networks [@he2016resnet] pre-trained on ImageNet [@deng2009imagenet], U-Net [@ronneberger2015unet], the Dice loss [@milletari2016dice], bootstrap resampling [@efron1979bootstrap] and Kendall's rank correlation [@kendall1938].

### 3.2 Audit procedure
Forged–authentic pairs were matched by 256-bit perceptual hash and verified by pixel difference (mean outside-mask difference below 25 grey levels and either hash distance ≤ 10 or fewer than 5 % of outside-mask pixels changed by more than 8), yielding 3,247/3,249 verified training pairs but only 89/812 test pairs. For every verified pair we computed the per-pixel maximum-channel absolute difference D = max_c |forged − authentic|, connected-component statistics of the mask, perceptual-hash neighbourhoods across splits, and — motivated by a visual inspection during the ground-truth verification (Sec. 4.3) — a *green-residue* detector: pixels with G − max(R, B) > 50 and G > 110 that are present in the forged image but not in its authentic source, counted inside a ±6 px ring around the box boundary.

### 3.3 Findings
(i) *Mask geometry.* All 4,061 masks are single rectangles (rectangularity 1.00); the metadata box equals the mask box in 4,047 cases, the remaining 12 rows have swapped provenance, so masks rather than metadata are used as ground truth throughout.
(ii) *Mask semantics.* The median fraction of box pixels with D > 8 is 0.49 (IQR 0.37–0.62) and the median IoU between {D > 8} and the box is 0.48 (Fig. 1); the paper's "pixel-precise" wording describes box precision.
(iii) *Outside-box edits.* 33.5 % of pairs have D > 8 pixels outside the box (Gemini 35.9 %, Ideogram 11.9 %; WildReceipt 60 %, CORD 0.4 %), typically the whole text line being re-synthesised; 48.6 % of pairs show a global ±1–3 difference over the entire image at D > 0 that vanishes for ε ≥ 4 (Sec. 4.1).
(iv) *Scale.* The median tampered area is 0.98 % of the image, the median mask height 33 px, and images range from 0.03 to 34.8 MP (Fig. A1), so native-resolution tiling is mandatory: resizing a 750×1,296 receipt to 512×512 leaves the median field 13 px high.
(v) *Leakage and negatives.* 72/812 test forgeries (8.9 %) share a source document with a training image (pHash ≤ 20); the released `TestingSet/authentic` folder mirrors the test forgeries for only 86 images, 254 of its files are byte-identical to training authentics and no SROIE authentic is included, so it cannot serve as the negative class for image-level evaluation.
(vi) *Confounds.* Generator and source are entangled (no Ideogram in WildReceipt, 45 % in XFUND) and Ideogram edits are smaller (median 0.27 % vs 1.07 % of the image).
(vii) *Green marker residue.* 2,514 of 3,336 verified pairs (75.4 %) contain at least 20 new saturated-green pixels in the ring around the box, and in 2,509 of them the residue forms a line-like component of 15 px or more. The residue is almost entirely *inside* the box (median 176 px inside, 0 outside; outside pixels in 0.3 % of affected pairs), and it is generator-specific: 83.6 % of Gemini pairs versus 1.5 % of Ideogram pairs (CORD 50 %, SROIE 86 %, WildReceipt 90 %, XFUND 51 %). It affects 93 % of the 89 verified test pairs. The pattern matches the pipeline described by the authors [@wu2026aiforgedoc] — a 3 px green rectangle drawn on the context crop to tell the editor where the field is, followed by paste-back of the field box only — with the inner edge of the drawn rectangle falling inside the pasted region. We verified visually that the line is absent from the authentic sources (Fig. 2).
(viii) *Licence.* The card states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with gating while `dataset_info.json` says CC BY 4.0; we treat the restrictive reading as binding and redistribute only identifier lists and derived masks.

### 3.4 Splits
The official test split (812) is untouched. Training images are divided by source-document groups — connected components of verified pair edges and perceptual-hash near-duplicate edges (Hamming ≤ 20) — into 2,762 train and 487 validation images with no group overlap; the 581 training groups that touch the test split are kept in training. Test results are reported on all 812 images and on the 740 "clean" images without a near-duplicate in training. A stratified pilot subset (400 train / 100 validation) was used for the mid-term feasibility study (Sec. 6.1).

## 4. Derived Ground Truths and Controls

### 4.1 Pixel-change and glyph ground truths
From D we derive (Table 2): **pixel_ε** = {D > ε} for ε ∈ {4, 8, 16}; **pixel_8_morph** = {D > 8} followed by a 3×3 opening, removal of components smaller than 8 px and a 3×3 closing; **glyph** = (Sauvola text pixels of forged ∪ authentic, window 25, k = 0.2) ∩ dilate({D > 8}, 7×7), with the stroke polarity decided per box (dark-on-light unless the Sauvola-dark ratio inside the box exceeds 0.5 — the naive median-brightness rule mis-fires on 199 dim photographs; the corrected rule, `glyph_v2`, inverts only 21 boxes); and **bbox ∩ pixel_8** as a conservative variant. The ε-sensitivity curve (Fig. 3) separates two phenomena: the share of pairs whose outside-box pixels change by more than 1 % of the box falls from 47.8 % at ε = 0 to 4.7 % at ε = 4 and 1.3 % at ε = 8 — the global re-encoding trace of the Gemini path — whereas the share with at least one outside-box pixel is still 33.5 % at ε = 8 and 12 % at ε = 16 — genuine local edits outside the box. Morphological cleaning reduces the number of pairs with outside-box components from 1,117 to 156. Ten pairs with D_max ≤ 4 (forged ≈ authentic) and five pairs whose only changes lie outside the box are excluded from training under every condition; the training conditions use **bbox**, **pixel_8_morph** ("pixel") and **glyph_v2** ("glyph").

### 4.2 Benign controls
From the authentic image of every validation forgery we construct (Table 3): (a) **benign paste-back** — the box expanded by 50 % per side (≥ 100 px) is up-sampled 2× with Lanczos, down-sampled back and only the box region is pasted onto the authentic copy, i.e., the pipeline's compositing step without content change and without the green marker; (b) **full-image resampling** — the whole image through the same 2× cycle; (c) PNG re-encoding (pixel-identical, kept as a null control). Benign paste-back leaves a median of 72 pixels with D > 8 (real forgeries: 3,020) confined to the box, whereas full-image resampling produces a comparable *count* of D > 8 pixels (median 2,627) spread over all text edges at much lower amplitude.

### 4.3 VLM-assisted verification of the derived masks
The protocol called for a 100-pair human verification. Because no annotator time was available before this version, we audited the stratified 100-pair sheet (7 source × generator strata) with a vision-language model (claude-sonnet-5 via the platform API) that received the six-panel image of Fig. 2 and the metadata values and returned a structured verdict. Of 96 parsed answers, the pixel-change mask was judged OK in 69 and PARTIAL in 26 cases (WRONG in 1), the glyph mask OK in 55 and PARTIAL in 41; the model judged that the box contained substantial unchanged area in 96/96 cases; it reported green-marked pixels outside the box in 21 cases and could confirm a visible change there in only 4 (15 unclear). Asked independently about a thin green line near the box edge, it answered yes for 40, no for 24 and unsure for 32 pairs; against the objective residue detector its yes/no answers agree in 45 of 64 decided cases (37 true positives, 16 misses, 3 false alarms), i.e., it under-reports the artefact. A repeat pass on 18 pairs agreed with the first on 83 % (pixel verdict), 67 % (glyph verdict), 83 % (outside-box) and 78 % (green line) of items, and in 68 % of 78 checkable cases the model's free-text description of the change contained the forged value from the metadata. We treat this as a consistency check of the derived masks, not as a substitute for human annotation; the sheet with both the automatic flags and the VLM columns is released for human completion.

## 5. Methods — and why they were chosen

This section is written so that each design decision can be traced to a property of the data established in Sections 3–4.

### 5.1 Problem formulation
Given a document image **x** ∈ ℝ^{H×W×3}, tampering localization predicts a map **p** ∈ [0,1]^{H×W}, the probability that each pixel was altered; thresholding **p** gives a binary mask. Image-level detection reduces **p** to a scalar (we use the mean of the top 1 % of pixel scores) or trains a classifier on crops. Because the dataset's masks are field boxes, the *target* of localization is itself a design choice; we train and evaluate under three targets (Sec. 4.1).

### 5.2 Classical pipeline (feature extraction + shallow classifiers)
*Why.* A hand-crafted baseline establishes whether low-level statistics alone separate edited from clean regions, and it connects the study to the course material. Features are computed on 256×256 grey-scale patches: statistics of the first 15 zig-zag AC coefficients of the 8×8 block DCT [@ahmed1974dct] (sensitive to JPEG block structure — expected *not* to help on PNG data, which the result confirms), a 10-bin uniform LBP histogram [@ojala2002lbp] (micro-texture of rendered vs photographed glyphs), four GLCM statistics [@haralick1973glcm] averaged over four directions (contrast, homogeneity, energy, correlation), Laplacian high-pass residual statistics (mean |r|, σ, kurtosis — the spatial-domain analogue of a noise residual), and radial FFT band-energy ratios (frequency-domain high-pass filtering in the sense of Gonzalez & Woods). Classifiers: RBF-SVM [@cortes1995svm] (standardised features, small C/γ grid) and a 500-tree random forest [@breiman2001rf], which also yields feature importances.

### 5.3 Patch classifier: ResNet-18 in three regimes
*Why.* Before investing in segmentation we asked whether a pre-trained network with a new head ("transfer learning") beats training from scratch on 1.6k patches — the course's "pretrained model + new layer" question. ResNet-18 [@he2016resnet] with ImageNet weights [@deng2009imagenet] is used (a) frozen with a new linear head (linear probe), (b) fully fine-tuned, (c) randomly initialised. Input: the 224×224 centre of a 256 native crop (no resizing, which would add a resampling trace of its own); no horizontal flips (text orientation); mild brightness/contrast jitter; binary cross-entropy; AdamW. Negatives are (i) clean crops of the same forged image and (ii) the *same-coordinate crop of the paired authentic image* — the latter is the strongest control because content and position are identical and only the manipulation differs.

### 5.4 Localizer: U-Net with a ResNet-18 encoder
*Why U-Net.* Fields are 33 px high on average; a decoder with skip connections [@ronneberger2015unet] restores the spatial resolution lost in the encoder, which is what small-region segmentation needs. *Why a ResNet-18 encoder.* With 2.7k training images, ImageNet initialisation shortens convergence and regularises; ResNet-18 (14.3 M parameters in the full U-Net) trains in 10 minutes per run on a 16 GB GPU, which made nine seeds × conditions affordable. *Why native-resolution crops.* Resizing destroys the signal (Sec. 3.3-iv); we train on 512×512 crops at native scale, 70 % centred on a randomly chosen ground-truth pixel (±128 px jitter, a quarter of the crop) and 30 % at random, two crops per image per epoch. *Why BCE + Dice.* Tampered pixels are ≈ 1 % of each image; Dice [@milletari2016dice] is insensitive to the negative majority and complements the per-pixel cross-entropy. *Why RGB only.* A pilot variant with two extra channels (FFT Gaussian high-pass, D₀ ≈ 20 px, and a 3×3 Laplacian residual) did not improve over RGB (Sec. 6.1), consistent with the literature's warning that frequency cues can hurt on PNG data [@chen2024ffdn]; adding them would also have confounded the ground-truth comparison. *Inference.* Full-image sliding window (512 px, stride 448, averaged overlaps); images with a longest side above 4,096 px (15 validation, 29 test) are scaled to 4,096 and the map resized back.

### 5.5 Training protocol and checkpoint selection
All conditions share AdamW (lr 1e-4), batch 16, bf16 mixed precision, 10 epochs (fixed before the runs), seeds 42–44, and early stopping on the validation AUPRC of crops *under the training ground truth*; only the best checkpoint is kept. The number of epochs was chosen from a one-epoch dry run (≈ 60 s per epoch including validation) to keep the 12-run grid under 2.5 GPU-hours. Section 6.2 reports convergence diagnostics (best epoch, last-three-epoch slope, train loss) for every run so that the reader can judge whether 10 epochs sufficed.

### 5.6 Evaluation: metrics and statistics
*Why threshold-free metrics first.* With a 1:100 class ratio the pixel AUROC is dominated by easy negatives and stays above 0.9 for almost any sensible model (Table 5); the area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC, pooled over all pixels and computed exactly from 256-bin histograms) and the per-image average precision (AP) are the informative threshold-free summaries. *Thresholded metrics.* F1 and IoU at 0.5, at the validation-calibrated global threshold τ* (grid 0.05–0.95), and the per-image Oracle-F1 (best threshold per image, an unattainable upper bound), following DOCFORGE-BENCH. *Dual ground truth.* Every model is scored against all three ground truths on validation; on the official test split only the box ground truth exists for 723 of 812 images. *Uncertainty.* Seeds are treated as a fixed factor (three-seed mean per image); confidence intervals come from 1,000 bootstrap resamples over source-document groups [@efron1979bootstrap], paired across conditions; p-values are two-sided bootstrap p-values with Holm correction within each hypothesis family; ranking stability uses Kendall's τ [@kendall1938]. The falsification criteria were fixed in the Phase-1 report before any full-data run.

### 5.7 Shortcut probes
Each probe isolates one candidate cue and includes a control that should *not* move a model that ignores that cue.
1. **Boundary ablation.** A 5 px ring around the box boundary (dilate 3 − erode 2) is Gaussian-blurred (σ = 1.5). *Placebo:* the same ring shape at a random non-overlapping position (should change nothing). *Interior:* erode(box, 3) is blurred and the boundary preserved (tests reliance on glyph content). Reported as relative ΔAUPRC/ΔAP on the same images.
2. **Rectangle fit.** The largest connected component of the thresholded prediction is replaced by its bounding box. If a metric does not punish this, it rewards rectangular shape rather than pixel accuracy. We also report the rectangularity (mask area / area of its bounding box) and component count of raw predictions.
3. **Benign controls.** False-positive pixel rate, share of images with > 0.1 % positive pixels and image-level AUROC (forged vs control) on authentic, benign paste-back and full-image-resampled validation images.
4. **Global-trace probe (H4).** A ResNet-18 trained to separate 224 px crops that do *not* touch the mask of a forged image from the same-coordinate crops of the authentic source; an AUROC above chance indicates image-wide evidence outside the field. *Positive control:* box-centred crops. *Symmetric control:* negatives replaced by full-image-resampled authentics.
5. **Marker probe (new).** For every validation pair the green residue (Sec. 3.3-vii, dilated by 1 px) is either *repaired* in the forged image by copying the authentic pixels (content of the forgery unchanged, marker removed) or *transferred* onto the authentic image (marker present, content unchanged). A model that uses the marker loses accuracy on the repaired forgery and predicts the box on the authentic-plus-marker image, where nothing was edited.

### 5.8 Zero-shot references
TruFor (official weights, ≤ 2 MP protocol of DOCFORGE-BENCH) and ADCD-Net (MIT licence; images re-encoded as JPEG Q100 and scaled to a 512 px long side as in the repository's cross-domain protocol, with an Otsu-based text mask replacing PaddleOCR) are run with the same evaluation code. They are *not* trained on AIForge-Doc and are reported for context in a separate table; the comparison is not on equal footing, which is stated wherever they appear.
